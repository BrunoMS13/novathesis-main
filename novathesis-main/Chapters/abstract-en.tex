The increasing frequency and intensity of wildfires, exacerbated by climate change, 
pose a growing threat to residential areas, particularly those surrounded by 
dense vegetation. Efficient risk assessment and mitigation strategies are critical 
for protecting homes and communities from fire hazards. This thesis explores the 
use of deep learning techniques to automate the classification of housing fire 
risk based on surrounding vegetation characteristics.

Leveraging a dataset of over 2,000 isolated houses in Continental Portugal, 
provided by the Association for the Development of Industrial Aerodynamics 
(ADAI), this research employs aerial imagery sourced from the open data API 
of the Direção-Geral do Território (DGT). Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) 
serve as the foundation for the classification model, with architectures such as 
ResNet-50, DenseNet-161, and EfficientNet-B5 evaluated for their effectiveness. 
A comparative analysis is conducted between models trained on RGB imagery and 
those incorporating additional spectral data, such as Infrared-Green-Red (IRG) 
and Normalized Difference Vegetation Index (NDVI), to assess their impact on 
classification accuracy.

To enhance model performance, data augmentation techniques are applied, and an 
ensemble learning approach is implemented to improve classification robustness 
across varying vegetation types. The proposed methodology streamlines risk 
assessment, reducing manual effort and enabling more efficient identification 
of fire-prone areas. Ultimately, this research contributes to the advancement 
of automated wildfire risk analysis, supporting proactive mitigation strategies 
and enhancing community resilience in fire-prone regions.
