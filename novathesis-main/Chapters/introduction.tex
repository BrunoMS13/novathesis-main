\section{Motivation}
In recent years, the impact of climate change has become increasingly visible in the form
of more frequent and severe weather events, including wildfires. These fires pose a significant
threat to ecosystems, properties, and human lives, particularly when they are close to
residential areas.

Wildfire risk reduction around residential areas is very dependent on effective
vegetation management in their surroundings. Recognizing the fire risk associated with
different types of vegetation is essential for planning preventative measures to safeguard
homes from the devastating impact of wildfires.

The Agency for the Development of Firefighting Initiatives (ADAI) has conducted a
comprehensive survey at a national level, covering over 2,000 isolated house locations to identify
various risk classes associated with the surrounding vegetation. This data is important
for assessing the potential fire hazards posed by vegetation around residential areas and
plays a crucial role in strategic fire management and community safety planning.

The traditional methods of assessing these risks are often labor-intensive and subject
to human error, involving extensive fieldwork and manual data analysis. On the other hand
machine learning, especially deep learning, offers a revolutionary approach to this challenge.
By automating the analysis of satellite and aerial imagery to classify vegetation risk,
machine learning can provide faster, more accurate assessments at scale. This capability is
particularly valuable for continuously updating our understanding of risk as the landscape
changes over time due to both human activity and natural processes.

\section{Objectives}
The primary goal of this dissertation is to develop a classifier using ML techniques that
can accurately identify vegetation risk classes near residences, based on satellite imagery
or aerial photographs, in accordance with ADAI's risk classification standards.
To achieve this, the dissertation will focus on gathering and preprocessing relevant data,
extracting and selecting key features, and developing and optimizing various machine learning models.

Additionally, this research aims to contribute to more effective and efficient wildfire
risk reduction strategies.
