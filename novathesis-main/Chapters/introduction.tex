\section{Motivation}

The increasing frequency and severity of wildfires, exacerbated by climate change, 
pose a growing threat to ecosystems, properties, and human lives, particularly in 
wildland-urban interface areas \cite{unep2022wildfires}. Effective wildfire 
mitigation requires proactive risk assessment and management strategies, 
particularly in fire-prone regions such as Portugal.

In response, Portugal has implemented strict vegetation management regulations 
to reduce fire hazards around residential areas. Decree-Law 124/2006 \cite{portugal2006}, 
updated by Decree-Law 82/2021 \cite{portugal2021}\cite{houserefuge2021}\cite{houserefuge2024}, 
mandates a 50-meter defensible space around structures, requiring vegetation reduction and 
clearance of at least 5 meters between trees and buildings, with a minimum of 4 meters 
between tree canopies, summarized in Figure \ref{fig:veg-man}.

\begin{figure}[ht]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=1\textwidth]{../Figures/Veg-management.png}
    \caption{Summary of legal requirements for vegetation management around buildings \cite{icnf1}\cite{icnf2}\cite{icnf3}.}
    \label{fig:veg-man}
\end{figure}

Traditional fire risk assessment methods rely on manual field surveys, which are 
labor-intensive, time-consuming, and susceptible to human error. Machine learning, 
particularly deep learning, offers a scalable and automated alternative by analyzing 
aerial imagery to classify vegetation risk levels. These technologies enable 
authorities to efficiently assess fire risks, enforce fuel management regulations, 
and adapt to landscape changes in real time, ultimately enhancing wildfire resilience 
and protecting communities.

\section{Background}

To improve fire risk assessment, systematic efforts have been undertaken, such as 
the national survey by ADAI, which analyzed approximately 2,000 isolated houses to 
evaluate vegetation-related fire risk \cite{almeida2020analise}. Houses were selected 
based on areas burned between 2016 and 2020, using data from the ICNF (Instituto de 
Conservação da Natureza e das Florestas), ensuring a geographically balanced sample.

Fire risk classification in this study focused on vegetation within a 50-meter 
radius of each house, using satellite imagery from 2018-2020. The assessment 
categorized natural fuels into spontaneous vegetation (wildland growth) and 
non-spontaneous vegetation (human-maintained areas like gardens or agricultural plots), 
further classifying vegetation into arboreal, shrub, and herbaceous types.

\begin{figure}[ht]
    \centering

The classification system defines five risk levels: low, moderate, high, very high, 
and extreme, based on vegetation type and its proximity to the structure. Only vegetation 
capable of sustaining and propagating fire was considered, meaning well-maintained, 
irrigated lawns were excluded from the analysis.



\section{Objectives}

The primary objective of this dissertation is to develop a robust machine learning classifier 
capable of accurately identifying vegetation risk classes near isolated residences based on 
high-resolution aerial imagery, aligned with the fire risk classification standards 
established by \gls{adai}. To achieve this, the study will leverage orthophotos provided 
by \gls{dgt}, alongside labeled data from \gls{adai}, ensuring a comprehensive dataset for 
training and evaluation.

This study will explore different deep learning architectures to determine the most 
effective approach for vegetation risk classification. A key focus is assessing the impact 
of different spectral inputs on classification performance. Specifically, this work will:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Compare the effectiveness of models trained exclusively on RGB imagery against those incorporating additional spectral data, such as IRG (Infrared-Green-Red) and NDVI (Normalized Difference Vegetation Index).
    \item Evaluate the trade-offs between classification accuracy and computational complexity when using different architectures, such as ResNet-50, DenseNet-161, and EfficientNet-B5.
    \item Implement and analyze an ensemble learning strategy to improve classification robustness across varying vegetation types.
\end{itemize}

By systematically comparing different spectral inputs and model architectures, this research 
aims to provide insights into the most effective methodologies for automated fire risk 
assessment, ultimately contributing to improved wildfire mitigation strategies.
