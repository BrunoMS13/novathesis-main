\chapter{Models and architecture}
\label{cha:models-architecture}

In this chapter, we discuss the models and architectures used to classify
the fire risk of isolated houses. We explore the different models and
their respective architectures, as well as the classification layer used
to predict the fire risk, we also show the ensembles architecture used
. It will also be explained the unfreezing layer
strategy used in the Densenet161 training.

\section{ResNet-50 Architecture}


ResNet-50 \cite{he2016deep} is a deep convolutional neural network from the ResNet family, 
which introduced residual learning to significantly enhance gradient propagation 
in deep architectures. The key innovation in ResNet models is the use of residual 
connections, which allow the network to learn residual functions rather than direct 
mappings. This mitigates the vanishing gradient problem and enables the training of 
very deep networks.

ResNet-50 consists of 50 layers, primarily composed of residual blocks. Each residual 
block incorporates identity shortcuts that bypass multiple convolutional layers, 
facilitating gradient flow and allowing for deeper feature learning without the risk of 
accuracy degradation.

\begin{figure}[h]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.5\textwidth]{../Figures/residual_block.png}
    \caption{Representation of a residual block used in ResNet-50 from \cite{he2016deep}.}
    \label{fig:resnet_block}
\end{figure}

The architecture follows a structured layout, beginning with an initial convolutional 
layer followed by multiple residual blocks. Each residual block as seen in Figure \ref{fig:resnet_block}
consists of a bottleneck 
structure with three convolutional layers: a $1 \times 1$ convolution for dimensionality 
reduction, a $3 \times 3$ convolution for feature extraction, and another $1 \times 1$ 
convolution for dimensionality restoration. Shortcut connections then add the original 
input back to the transformed output, improving feature reuse and enabling efficient 
deep learning.

The model takes an input of size $224 \times 224 \times 3$ and progressively reduces 
spatial dimensions while increasing feature depth. A final global average pooling 
layer extracts high-level features before passing them to a fully connected layer for classification.

    \centering

        Pooling          & $56 \times 56 \times 64$   & $3 \times 3$ max pooling \\

        Residual Block 3 & $14 \times 14 \times 1024$ & $\left[1\times1, 3\times3, 1\times1\right] \times 6$ \\

        Fully Connected  & $1 \times 1 \times C$      & Classification layer \\
        \bottomrule
    \end{tabular}
    \caption{Layer-wise structure of ResNet-50.}
    \label{tab:resnet_layers}
\end{table}


A major advantage of ResNet-50 is its ability to train deep models efficiently 
without suffering from performance degradation. The residual connections enable 
stable gradient flow, reducing the risk of vanishing gradients and improving 
convergence speed. These properties make ResNet-50 particularly effective for 
large-scale image classification tasks.

Additionally, ResNet-50 incorporates batch normalization and ReLU activation to enhance 
training stability and efficiency. Its hierarchical structure captures increasingly 
complex patterns with depth, making it well-suited for high-resolution image analysis.

ResNet-50 has demonstrated strong performance on benchmark datasets such as 
ImageNet and has become a standard architecture in deep learning applications. 
Its combination of depth, residual learning, and efficient feature extraction 
makes it a powerful model for image classification, object detection, and other 
computer vision tasks.


\section{DenseNet-161 Architecture}

DenseNet-161 \cite{huang2017densely} is a deep convolutional neural network from the DenseNet family, 
designed to optimize feature reuse and improve information flow between layers. 
The core principle of DenseNet is dense connectivity, where each layer receives 
input from all preceding layers, ensuring more effective gradient propagation 
and reducing feature redundancy.

\begin{figure}[ht]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.8\textwidth]{../Figures/DensenetArchitect.png}
    \caption{Representation of DenseNet with three dense blocks from \cite{DensenetResearchGate2025}.}
    \label{fig:densenet_block}
\end{figure}

A defining characteristic of DenseNet-161 is the use of dense blocks, each 
consisting of multiple convolutional layers that share information through 
direct connections, as illustrated in Fig. \ref{fig:densenet_block}. These dense 
connections alleviate the vanishing gradient problem and enable the model to 
achieve high accuracy with fewer parameters compared to traditional deep architectures. 
Between these dense blocks, transition layers are used to control feature map growth, 
reducing computational cost while maintaining effective feature extraction.

DenseNet-161 follows a structured layout, beginning with an initial convolutional 
layer followed by four dense blocks. Each dense block consists of multiple layers 
applying $3 \times 3$ convolutions with batch normalization and ReLU activation. 
Within a dense block, each layer concatenates its feature maps with those of all 
preceding layers, increasing the model's representational capacity. Between the 
dense blocks, transition layers with $1 \times 1$ convolutions and average pooling 
help control dimensionality and prevent excessive memory consumption.

The model takes an input of size $224 \times 224 \times 3$ and progressively 
reduces spatial dimensions while increasing feature depth. The final global 
average pooling layer extracts high-level features, which are then passed to 
a fully connected classification layer.

One of DenseNet-161's key advantages is its efficient parameter utilization. 
Unlike traditional deep networks, which may contain redundant parameters, DenseNet 
maximizes feature reuse through its densely connected layers, leading to improved
accuracy with fewer parameters. This makes it particularly effective for applications 
requiring robust feature extraction with limited computational resources.

Additionally, DenseNet-161 incorporates residual-like connections without summation, 
preserving fine-grained information across layers. The inclusion of transition layers 
ensures that the number of feature maps does not grow excessively, keeping computational 
costs manageable while maintaining high representational capacity.

Due to its strong performance on benchmark datasets such as ImageNet, DenseNet-161 
has been widely adopted in image classification tasks. Its dense connectivity, batch 
normalization, and efficient pooling mechanisms make it well-suited for tasks that 
require deep feature learning. In this study, we leverage DenseNet-161's strengths 
to enhance fire risk classification, capitalizing on its superior feature extraction 
capabilities to improve predictive performance.



\section{EfficientNet-B5 Architecture}

EfficientNet-B5 \cite{efficientnet} is a deep convolutional neural network from the EfficientNet family, 
designed to achieve high accuracy while maintaining computational efficiency. Unlike 
traditional deep networks that scale depth, width, or resolution independently, 
EfficientNet models employ compound scaling, which balances these three factors proportionally. 
This results in a more optimized architecture that improves accuracy without a 
significant increase in computational cost.

The core principle behind compound scaling is that increasing the network's depth 
(number of layers), width (number of channels per layer), and resolution (input image size) 
together leads to more efficient model scaling compared to arbitrarily increasing one 
of these dimensions. EfficientNet-B5 follows this principle by scaling up EfficientNet-B0 
using pre-determined coefficients, resulting in a larger, more accurate model with a 
manageable computational requirement.


EfficientNet-B5 utilizes Mobile Inverted Bottleneck Convolution (MBConv) blocks, 
originally introduced in MobileNetV2 \cite{mobilenetv2}. These blocks employ depthwise separable 
convolutions, reducing the number of parameters while preserving feature extraction 
efficiency. Each MBConv block consists of:
\begin{itemize}
    \item An expansion phase to increase dimensionality,
    \item A depthwise convolution for spatial feature extraction,
    \item A projection phase to reduce dimensionality while maintaining essential information.
\end{itemize}
Additionally, squeeze-and-excitation (SE) blocks are integrated into each MBConv block, 
enabling the network to adaptively recalibrate feature maps by learning channel-wise attention.

The network begins with a standard convolutional layer, followed by multiple MBConv 
blocks, progressively increasing feature depth while reducing spatial resolution. The 
final layers consist of a global average pooling operation followed by a fully 
connected classification layer.

detailing the key operations at each stage of the network. The columns represent the specific

and the number of times the operation is repeated.

One of the key advantages of EfficientNet-B5 is its ability to achieve high accuracy 
while maintaining computational efficiency. Unlike traditional deep networks, which 
often increase parameters disproportionately, EfficientNet achieves state-of-the-art 
performance on benchmark datasets with significantly fewer parameters and FLOPs.

Additionally, EfficientNet-B5 incorporates the following:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Swish activation functions to improve gradient flow,
    \item Batch normalization for enhanced training stability,
    \item Squeeze-and-excitation (SE) blocks for adaptive feature recalibration.
\end{itemize}
These enhancements enable EfficientNet-B5 to achieve superior accuracy on higher resolution 
images ($456 \times 456$), leading to better feature extraction for fine-grained classification tasks.

EfficientNet-B5 has consistently outperformed many traditional deep networks on ImageNet 
and other large-scale classification benchmarks, demonstrating its effectiveness in real-world 
applications. Its combination of depthwise separable convolutions, squeeze-and-excitation blocks, 
and compound scaling makes it one of the most efficient architectures for high-accuracy 
image classification. These properties make EfficientNet-B5 a strong candidate for fire 
risk classification, as it balances computational efficiency with strong feature extraction 
capabilities.


\section{Custom Classification Layer}

To adapt the pre-trained architectures for the fire risk classification task, the default 
fully connected (FC) layers of DenseNet-161, ResNet-101, and EfficientNet-B5 were 
replaced with a custom classification head. This modification enhances the network's 
ability to generalize while improving flexibility in learning complex patterns.

Table \ref{tab:classification_layer} presents the architecture 
of the custom classification layer.

    \centering
    \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.3} % Adjust row height
    \setlength{\tabcolsep}{1pt} % Adjust column spacing
    \begin{tabular}{lcc}
        \toprule

        \midrule
        Fully Connected (FC1) & Input: extracted features, Output: 4096 & ReLU \\
        Dropout & Probability: 0.4 & - \\
        Fully Connected (FC2) & Input: 4096, Output: 4096 & ReLU \\
        Dropout & Probability: 0.4 & - \\
        Output Layer (Binary Classification) & Input: 4096, Output: 1 & Sigmoid \\
        \bottomrule
    \end{tabular}
    \caption{Structure of the custom classification layer.}
    \label{tab:classification_layer}
\end{table}

The custom classification layer consists of two fully connected (FC) layers with 
ReLU activation, followed by dropout regularization to improve generalization. 
The final layer outputs a single neuron with a Sigmoid activation function for 
binary classification.

The dropout rate of 0.4 was assumed based on standard deep learning practices, 
aiming to prevent overfitting while preserving meaningful feature representations. 

By integrating this custom classification layer, the model preserves the rich feature 
representations extracted by the deep feature extractor while enhancing adaptability 
to the fire risk classification task.


\section{Ensemble Model}

To improve classification performance, two ensemble models were explored: one using only 
\gls{rgb} input and another incorporating multiple input types. The motivation behind these 
ensembles was to leverage different deep learning architectures to enhance robustness 
and generalization.

\subsection{\gls{rgb}-Only Ensemble}

The first ensemble model, illustrated in Figure \ref{fig:rgb_ensemble}, was designed 
using only \gls{rgb} images as input. While additional spectral information, such as near-infrared 
(NIR) bands or vegetation indices like \gls{ndvi}, can be beneficial, \gls{rgb} data is often the most 
accessible and widely available source of imagery. In many real-world scenarios, obtaining 
\gls{irg} images or computing \gls{ndvi} may not always be feasible due to sensor limitations or data 
availability. Therefore, this model serves as a baseline to evaluate how well fire risk 
can be classified using only standard \gls{rgb} imagery.

In this approach, \gls{rgb} images are processed through three different pre-trained architectures: 
DenseNet-161, ResNet-50, and EfficientNet-B5. The predictions from these models are then 
merged to produce the final classification result using a majority voting strategy, where 
the final decision is determined based on the most frequently predicted class.

\begin{figure}[ht]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.8\textwidth]{../Figures/EnsembleRGBOnly.drawio.pdf}
    \caption{\gls{rgb}-based ensemble model combining DenseNet-161, ResNet-50, and EfficientNet-B5.}
    \label{fig:rgb_ensemble}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Multi-Input Ensemble}

The second ensemble model, shown in Figure \ref{fig:multi_input_ensemble}, was designed 
to incorporate multiple input types and leverage additional spectral information. 
This approach considers three different input types: \gls{rgb} images, \gls{irg} 
images, and \gls{rgb} + \gls{ndvi} images.

The \gls{rgb} and \gls{irg} inputs were processed separately through DenseNet-161, 
ResNet-50, and EfficientNet-B5. To incorporate \gls{ndvi} information, the \gls{irg} 
images were used to extract the \gls{ndvi} channel, which was then combined with \gls{rgb} 
to create an additional \gls{rgb} + \gls{ndvi} input. This third input was processed 
only by DenseNet-161 and EfficientNet-B5, as ResNet-50 did not yield favorable 
results with this configuration.

As in the \gls{rgb}-only ensemble, the outputs from all networks were merged using 
majority voting, ensuring that the final classification decision was determined 
based on the consensus among the models.
